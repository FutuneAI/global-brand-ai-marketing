# The B2B AI Procurement Model: Five Stages and Content Coverage Framework

**Part of:** [AI-Era Marketing for Global Brands](../README.md) — Phase 2, Document 4

**Full argument:** [B2B Buyers Use AI to Make Decisions, Not Just Research Them](https://medium.com/@futuneai) · Medium

## Summary

B2B buyers use AI at multiple stages of procurement, not only at the discovery stage. The determining factor for brand visibility is not whether a brand appears in AI answers in general, but at which procurement stages its content is systematically retrievable.

This document defines the five-stage model and provides the operational criteria for diagnosing and closing coverage gaps.

---

## Definition: The Five-Stage AI Procurement Model

This model abstracts how AI tends to be used across B2B procurement. Real processes are non-linear and involve multiple stakeholders moving in parallel. The value of the model is the coverage question it surfaces: at which query types is a brand's content systematically retrievable?

| Stage | Label | Typical query type | Who submits it |
|-------|-------|-------------------|----------------|
| 1 | Category Research | "What options exist in this category?" | Procurement manager, researcher |
| 2 | Criteria Definition | "What should we evaluate? Which standards apply?" | Technical lead, procurement manager |
| 3 | Vendor Comparison | "How does Vendor A compare to Vendor B for use case X?" | Technical evaluator, buying team |
| 4 | Risk / Technical Evaluation | "Is this vendor credible? Any compliance or delivery risk?" | Technical evaluator, senior decision-maker |
| 5 | Internal Justification | "Help me build the business case for this vendor." | Internal champion, committee presenter |

---

## Coverage Status by Stage

| Stage | Typical brand coverage | Why |
|-------|----------------------|-----|
| 1 — Category Research | Systematic | Default content strategy addresses "who we are and what we do" |
| 2 — Criteria Definition | Absent or thin | Requires educational content that does not promote the brand directly |
| 3 — Vendor Comparison | Partial, unstructured | Brands publish capability claims but rarely publish comparison-framing content |
| 4 — Risk Evaluation | Thin | Requires third-party sources; owned content carries lower signal here |
| 5 — Internal Justification | Absent (emerging in tech/consulting/finance) | Requires structured, citable material usable in internal documents |

**Operational rule:** Coverage gaps at Stages 2, 4, and 5 are structural, not incidental. They result from content strategies designed for Stage 1 query patterns.

---

## Structural Gaps Explained

| Stage | Gap type | Root cause |
|-------|----------|-----------|
| 2 | Content gap | Criteria content does not promote the brand; most marketing teams are not resourced to produce it |
| 4 | Distribution gap | Third-party coverage corpus is thin; especially for Chinese suppliers in English-language AI systems |
| 5 | Format gap | Generic brand content is not usable in internal business cases; structured, citable material is required |

---

## Stage 2 Content: What Retrieves vs. What Does Not

Stage 2 has the highest leverage because criteria content is retrieved across multiple stages: it shapes Stage 2 queries, informs Stage 3 comparisons, and is usable as source material at Stage 5.

**Content that retrieves for Stage 2 queries:**
- Documents explaining how to evaluate suppliers in a specific category
- Application-specific standards and regulatory requirements by market
- Technical thresholds that distinguish qualified from unqualified suppliers
- Deployment context variables specific to defined use cases

**Content that does not retrieve for Stage 2 queries:**
- Product pages and capability overviews
- Case studies without the evaluation criteria context
- FAQ pages structured around "why choose us" rather than "how to evaluate this category."

## Multi-Stakeholder Coverage Matrix

Different members of a buying committee submit different query types. A brand optimized for one persona may be absent from the others.

| Buyer role | Typical AI use stage | Query focus |
|------------|---------------------|-------------|
| Procurement manager | Stage 1, Stage 2 | Category landscape, evaluation criteria |
| Technical evaluator | Stage 2, Stage 3, Stage 4 | Standards, technical comparison, compliance |
| Finance lead | Stage 4, Stage 5 | Risk, cost justification, ROI framing |
| Internal champion | Stage 5 | Business case structure, vendor comparison summary |

**Operational rule:** The question is not only "which stages are covered" but "for which buyer role, at which stage, is content systematically retrievable."

## Investment Priority Framework

| Priority | Stage focus | Rationale |
|----------|------------|-----------|
| 1 — Start here | Stage 2: Criteria Definition | Retrieves across Stages 2, 3, and 5; one document has upstream effects |
| 2 | Stage 4: Risk Validation | Earn third-party coverage; publish case studies with verifiable data |
| 3 | Stage 3: Vendor Comparison | Publish comparison-framing content with specific, application-contextualized claims |
| 4 — Maintain | Stage 1: Category Research | Already partially covered; incremental return is lower than Stages 2 and 4 |
